# What is Jewber?

Jewber is a ride app with exactly one driver, me, and a handful of riders, my friends. It works like Uber: you ask for a ride on a map, my phone rings, I accept, you watch my car come to you live, and you pay and rate me at the end. The difference is that it's just for us, and it's Jewish all the way through.

## Who can use it

Only people I've added. I put your email in my list, and you get an email from Jewber with the link. If you found Jewber some other way, the sign-in screen has "New here? Ask to join": you leave your name, I get a message, and I say yes or no. If it's yes, the same invitation email goes out.

## Signing in

There's no password. You type your email and Jewber sends you a 6-digit code, which works for 10 minutes. Once you're in, you stay signed in on that phone or computer until you sign out (Account → Sign out).

## Getting the app

- **Android:** download the APK from the newest release on GitHub, or from the Android app row in your Account, and open it. Android asks once to allow installs from your browser, which is normal for apps that don't come from the Play Store. If you use Obtainium, add the GitHub repo and the app updates itself. The app is better than the website on Android, because it keeps your ride in a notification ("on the way", "is here", "You're here") so you don't have to keep it open.
- **iPhone:** open the site in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. It installs like an app with its own icon, and it can send you notifications from there.
- **A computer:** it's a website, so any browser works.

## What's where

The home screen is a map with me on it when I'm on duty (roughly where I am, until I'm coming to get you), and a sheet with the greeting, how far away I am, "Where to?", and your places. Along the bottom are Home, Rides (your history and anything booked), Tab (what you owe and how you like to pay), and Account (you, your places, light or dark, sounds, notifications).
